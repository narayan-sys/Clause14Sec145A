The one idea that makes Clause 14(b) click

Books stay exclusive. Only the computation of income goes inclusive.
Section 145A does not require the assessee to change its books. AS-2 / Ind AS 2 in fact prohibit the inclusive method. So the assessee keeps GST out of purchase, sale and inventory in the books, and the tax auditor prepares a memorandum working showing what the figures would be on the inclusive basis. The difference between the two is what goes in the Clause 14(b) table. Ref: Guidance Note (Revised 2026), paras 26.8, 26.10, 26.15.
For a correctly maintained assessee the NET effect is normally NIL — and that is the expected answer, not a failure.
The GST added to purchases is recovered through ITC; the ITC relating to goods still in stock is what remains inside closing stock. Algebraically the four legs cancel. The Guidance Note states expressly that the section 145A adjustments have no impact on the trading account / gross profit. Ref: Guidance Note (Revised 2026), para 26.16.
Where the answer is NOT nil — almost always one of five things: (1) blocked ITC under s.17(5) that was not added to cost in the books; (2) Rule 42 / Rule 43 reversals not loaded back into cost; (3) non-refundable duties (e.g. basic customs duty) left out of inventory cost; (4) GST on goods in transit / stock lying with third parties not carried in inventory; (5) an inconsistency between opening and closing treatment. The tool isolates each of these separately so the auditor can see why a figure appears.
Auditor judgement flag — services. Post-amendment section 145A refers to purchase and sale of goods or services, and the Guidance Note records that it covers services and securities as well as goods. Two defensible positions exist for a pure service entity with no inventory and no WIP: (i) there is nothing to value, so Clause 14(a) and (b) are N.A.; and (ii) the services limb must still be tested even though the outcome is normally nil because output GST and input GST are both routed through balance-sheet accounts. This tool presents both and requires the auditor to record a reasoned conclusion. It will not manufacture a GST adjustment merely because a service company pays GST on inputs.
